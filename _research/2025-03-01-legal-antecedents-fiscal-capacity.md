abstract: >-
  This project studies a low-capacity state, the D.R. Congo, seeking to establish
  legal capacity and how its efforts to do so shape citizens' demand for the state.
  We examine the randomized rollout of a legal capacity-building program
  implemented at scale in the city of Kananga by the Provincial Ministry of
  Justice. Local property disputes are randomly assigned to receive free
  arbitration offered by state-licensed lawyers. We study how this legal capacity
  program shapes property rights security, social cohesion and trust, and
  citizens' willingness to fund the formal state through taxes. An information
  treatment helps shed light on mechanisms.
